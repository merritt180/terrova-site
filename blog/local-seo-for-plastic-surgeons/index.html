Local SEO for Plastic Surgeons: How to Get Found by Patients in Your City

Nobody chooses a plastic surgeon on impulse. A patient thinking about a facelift or a breast augmentation will spend weeks searching, reading, comparing, and quietly judging every practice in town before they ever pick up the phone. Your job is to be in the room for every one of those searches.

For an established practice, a single consultation can turn into a procedure worth thousands of dollars, and a happy patient sends friends your way for years. That is why local search deserves real attention. Here is how it works for a surgeon in a market like Los Angeles, Beverly Hills, or Newport Beach.

How patients actually search

Most patients start with a procedure and a place. They type "rhinoplasty surgeon Beverly Hills" or "tummy tuck Los Angeles" or "best plastic surgeon near me." Google answers with a map showing three practices, followed by organic results. The three map spots capture most of the calls, so that is the first place to compete.

Patients also search by name once they have a shortlist. They look up the surgeon, read reviews, and check the website. If what they find is thin or inconsistent, the practice drops off the list quietly. You never even know you lost them.

Start with a fully built Google Business Profile

Your profile is the engine behind map rankings. Choose "Plastic Surgeon" as the primary category, then add the secondary categories that truly describe your work. List the procedures you perform as services. Fill in the description with plain language about what you do and who you serve, and keep hours and phone number exactly right.

Photos matter more here than in almost any other field. Show the building, the consultation room, the surgical suite, and the team. Patients are deciding whether they feel comfortable walking through your door, and good photos answer that before they arrive.

Give every procedure its own page

This is the single most common gap we see on surgeon websites. One page titled "Procedures" with a list of names cannot rank for anything specific. A dedicated page for rhinoplasty, another for breast augmentation, another for facelift, and so on gives Google a clear match for each search.

Each page should explain who the procedure is for, what the consultation looks like, how recovery works, and what the surgeon's approach is. Mention your city naturally. Answer the questions patients actually ask, such as how long the downtime is and what results look like over time. Pages written that way earn rankings and also help patients feel ready to book.

Reviews carry extra weight in this field

A patient trusting a surgeon with their face or body reads reviews closely, and Google reads them too. Ratings, volume, and recency all play a role. A practice with 150 steady reviews will usually beat one with 40 older ones.

Ask every satisfied patient for a review at a natural moment, such as the follow up visit when they are feeling good about their results. Make it easy with a direct link. Reply to every review in a calm, professional tone, and never include private health details in a response. Privacy rules apply to what you say publicly, so keep replies general and warm.

Build trust signals into the website

Patients want proof of credentials before they commit. Put board certification, hospital affiliations, years of experience, and professional memberships where visitors can see them quickly. A clear surgeon biography with a real photo does more than most paid advertising.

Before and after galleries are valuable, but follow your medical board and platform advertising rules and always use properly consented images. A thoughtful gallery organized by procedure supports both rankings and conversions.

Keep your information consistent everywhere

Many surgeons practice at more than one location or have moved offices over the years. Old addresses on directories, hospital pages, and health listings confuse Google and confuse patients. Pick one exact format for your practice name, address, and phone number, then update every listing to match. If you operate from several locations, each one needs its own profile and its own page.

Make booking effortless

Ranking is only half the job. A patient who finds you at ten at night should be able to request a consultation in under a minute. A clear button on every page, a short form, and a phone number that is easy to tap on a mobile screen all help. A fast website matters too, since most of these searches happen on a phone.

The bottom line

Local SEO for a plastic surgery practice comes down to being visible when patients search, credible when they read, and easy to book when they decide. A complete profile, a page for every procedure, steady reviews, and consistent information do most of the heavy lifting. Practices that handle those four things well usually see steadier consultation requests within a few months.
