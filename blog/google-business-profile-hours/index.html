Google Business Profile Hours: How to Set Regular and Holiday Hours the Right Way

A prospective patient searches for a dentist at 7:45 on a Tuesday morning. Your listing says you open at 8. Your office actually opens at 7:30. Google shows you as closed, so she taps the next result and books there instead. You never knew she looked.

Business hours feel like the most boring field on your Google Business Profile, but they affect rankings, calls, and reviews more than most owners expect. Here is how to get them right, including the holiday season that is only a few weeks away.

Why hours matter more than you think

Google wants to send people to businesses that are open and ready to help. When someone searches "dentist open now" or "luxury car rental near me," Google filters and favors listings that show as open. If your hours are wrong or missing, you can disappear from those searches entirely.

Wrong hours also create reviews you never wanted. A customer drives across town, finds a locked door, and leaves one star with the comment "closed during posted hours." That review stays on your profile for years, and it was caused by a ten minute mistake.

Set your regular hours first

Log in to your profile, open the hours section, and enter the real hours your business answers the phone or welcomes people in. A few details that trip people up:

Use the hours customers can actually reach you. A law firm that lists 9 to 5 but lets calls go to voicemail at noon is setting up a bad first impression. List the hours someone will truly be there.

Add split hours if you close for lunch. Many dental practices and boutique offices close from 12 to 1. Google lets you add two time blocks in one day, so you can show 8 to 12 and 1 to 5 instead of pretending you are open straight through.

Mark days you are closed as closed. Do not leave a day blank. A blank day looks like missing information, and Google may treat the profile as less complete.

Think about appointment only businesses. A luxury car rental company that works by reservation can still list the hours the team answers calls and handles pickups. If you also offer after hours delivery or late returns, mention that in your description and your posts so customers know.

Use special hours for holidays

This is the step most businesses skip, and it is the one that causes the most trouble. Google has a separate special hours feature built for holidays and one time changes. It overrides your regular hours for specific dates, then goes back to normal on its own.

Look at the calendar now. Thanksgiving, the days around Christmas, New Year's Eve, and New Year's Day all change schedules for most businesses. A med spa that closes early on December 24th, a real estate team that skips Thanksgiving Friday, a dental practice that shuts down the week between Christmas and New Year's, all of them should set special hours at least two weeks ahead.

Google sometimes shows a "Hours might differ" label on listings around holidays when the business has not confirmed its schedule. That label makes people hesitate. Setting special hours removes it and tells customers you are paying attention.

Do not forget the details people check

Hours show up in more places than the main profile. Make sure they match everywhere:

Your website. If your site footer says one thing and your profile says another, customers notice and Google notices too.

Other directories. Yelp, Apple Maps, Bing, and industry listings all pull from different sources. When your hours differ across them, trust drops. Our post on online directories and citations explains how to keep these in sync.

Your phone greeting and voicemail. If your profile promises you are open, someone should answer, or the greeting should clearly say when you will call back.

Your social profiles. Facebook and Instagram have their own hours fields, and they are often the ones that go stale.

Say it in a post as well

Special hours are easy to miss, so back them up with a short Google Business post. Something simple like "Our office will be closed Thursday and Friday for Thanksgiving and will reopen Monday at 8" does two jobs. It informs customers, and it adds fresh activity to your profile. If you want ideas for what else to post, see our guide to Google Business posts.

A quick monthly check

Hours change more often than owners realize. A new hire changes the front desk schedule, a second location shifts its weekend coverage, or a seasonal slowdown cuts a late evening. Put a recurring reminder on your calendar to look at your profile on the first of each month. It takes two minutes, and it protects your calls, your reviews, and your ranking.

The bottom line

Accurate hours are a small detail that sends a big signal. They tell Google you run an organized business, and they tell customers they can count on you. Set your regular hours carefully, add special hours before every holiday, and keep every listing consistent. It is one of the cheapest improvements you can make to how you show up on Google.
