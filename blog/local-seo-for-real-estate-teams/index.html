Local SEO for Real Estate Teams: How Sellers Choose Who to Call

Most sellers interview one agent. They tell themselves they will talk to three, then they search "realtor in Sherman Oaks," look at the map, read a handful of reviews, and call the name that feels the most established. The interview is really a confirmation of a decision that already happened on a phone screen.

That is uncomfortable if you are a good agent with a thin online footprint. It is very good news if you are willing to build one, because most teams in your market have not. They rely on the brokerage website, a Zillow profile, and referrals. That leaves the local search results wide open.

Your brokerage page is not your business listing

Plenty of agents assume they are covered because their name appears on the brokerage site. Google does not see it that way. The brokerage ranks for the brokerage. You need your own Google Business Profile, listed as a real estate agent or real estate agency, tied to an address you can actually receive mail at.

If you run a team, list the team. If you work solo, list yourself. Either way, this listing is what puts you in the map pack, and the map pack is where the calls come from. Pick the primary category deliberately. "Real Estate Agent" and "Real Estate Agency" pull different results, and "Property Management Company" will drag you into searches you do not want.

Reviews decide who looks safe

Selling a house is the largest financial decision most people make. They are not comparing commission splits at the search stage. They are scanning for reassurance. A team with 60 reviews averaging 4.9 stars reads as safe. A team with 7 reviews reads as new, even if you have been closing deals for fifteen years.

The fix is a habit, not a campaign. Ask for the review at closing, when the client is happiest and you are standing right in front of them. Send the direct link by text before you leave. Waiting a week costs you most of them.

One more thing worth doing: reply to every review, including the short ones. A public reply that mentions the neighborhood and the type of transaction gives Google useful context and gives the next seller something to read.

Neighborhoods are the keywords

Agents tend to market themselves by city. Buyers and sellers search by neighborhood. Nobody types "Los Angeles homes for sale" when they mean Toluca Lake. They type Toluca Lake.

This is the biggest opportunity most teams are sitting on. Build a real page for each neighborhood you genuinely work in. Not a thin page with a map embed and a search widget, but a page that says something only someone who works there would know: what the streets are like, what inventory tends to move, what a typical sale looks like, what the schools and commute mean in practice.

Five honest neighborhood pages will outperform thirty auto-generated ones. Google has gotten very good at telling the difference, and so has the seller reading it.

Sold listings are proof, so use them

Every closed deal is a piece of content you already paid for. A short write-up with the neighborhood, the property type, and what happened in the sale does two jobs at once. It tells Google you are active in that area, and it shows a nervous seller that you have handled a house like theirs.

Keep it simple. A few paragraphs, the photos you already have, the neighborhood named in the headline. Post one or two a month and after a year you have a body of local proof that no competitor can copy.

Photos and updates keep the listing alive

Google favors profiles that show recent activity. For a real estate team this is easy, because you are photographing property constantly. Add fresh images to your profile regularly, use the posts feature to share a new listing or an open house, and keep your hours accurate.

A listing that has not been touched in eight months quietly slides down the results while the agent wonders why the phone stopped ringing.

Make your contact information identical everywhere

Agents accumulate profiles. Zillow, Realtor.com, the brokerage directory, an old team site, a Facebook page from 2019. If your phone number or business name is written differently across those, Google trusts your listing less.

Pick one exact version of your name, address, and phone number and make every profile match it, character for character. It is dull work and it moves rankings more reliably than almost anything else on this list.

The bottom line

Real estate is a referral business that now gets checked online before the referral is honored. Someone hands your name to a friend, and that friend searches you before calling. What they find in those ten seconds decides whether the referral survives.

Own your listing, collect reviews steadily, write like someone who actually knows the neighborhood, and keep everything consistent. Most teams in your market are doing none of this, which is exactly why it works.
