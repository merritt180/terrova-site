How to Tell If Your Local SEO Is Actually Working

Most owners judge their online marketing by one thing: how busy the week felt. That is a terrible measuring stick. A slow week at a dental practice might be a holiday. A busy month at a law firm might be one referral that had nothing to do with search. If you want to know whether the work is paying off, you need a few numbers you check on purpose.

Here is the short list that tells the truth, the numbers that only look impressive, and a routine that takes about fifteen minutes a month.

Start at the end: calls, forms, and booked appointments

The only number your bank account cares about is how many real people reached out. Everything else is a step on the way there. So before you look at rankings or traffic, count contacts.

Your Google Business Profile already tracks calls placed from your listing, clicks to your website, and requests for directions. Your website should track form submissions. If you take bookings online, count completed bookings. Write those four numbers down each month. A med spa that went from nine calls in June to twenty two in September has an answer, no interpretation required.

One warning. If the calls are up but the schedule is not, the problem has moved from marketing to the front desk. That is worth knowing too, and plenty of practices discover it here.

Read the profile insights the right way

Inside your Google Business Profile you can see how people found you. Google splits searches into two buckets. Direct searches are people typing your business name. Discovery searches are people typing what they need, like "estate planning attorney Encino" or "exotic car rental near me," and finding you anyway.

Direct searches mostly reflect your reputation and your existing customers. Discovery searches are the number that proves local SEO is working. When discovery climbs month after month, you are reaching people who had never heard of you. That is new business by definition.

Check your map ranking from the neighborhoods you care about

Rankings are not a single number. Google shows different map results depending on where the searcher is standing. A custom home builder in Calabasas might sit in the top three for people searching a mile away and fall off the map entirely for people in Woodland Hills. Checking your rank from your own office, on your own phone, while logged into your own Google account, tells you almost nothing.

The useful version is a grid. You check the same search from a spread of points across the area you actually serve, then watch that picture change over time. When the green zone widens, your reach is widening, and you will feel it in the phone before long.

Watch review pace, not just review count

Total reviews matter, but the pace matters more than most owners realize. Google pays attention to recency, and so do customers. A practice sitting at 180 reviews with nothing new since spring looks stale next to a competitor at 90 reviews who added six last month.

So track two things. How many new reviews came in this month, and how many of them you responded to. If the answer to the second question is not all of them, that is the easiest fix on this entire list.

Look at which pages people actually land on

If you have Google Search Console connected to your site, and you should, it will show you the searches that brought people to each page. This is where you find out whether the work is reaching the right buyers.

A law firm that suddenly gets traffic for "trust administration attorney" is being found by people with a specific, high value problem. A home builder getting found for "ADU builder Los Angeles" is reaching people with a budget and a timeline. Those are far better signs than an overall traffic bump, because they tell you what kind of person is arriving.

The numbers that look good and mean little

A few metrics get quoted in reports because they climb easily. Total impressions is the worst offender, because impressions rise anytime Google shows your listing to anyone for any reason, including searches that will never turn into a customer. Total website visits can be inflated by a single social post. Keyword rankings pulled from one location, as covered above, are close to meaningless on their own.

None of these are lies. They are just too easy to move without moving the business. If a report leads with them and never gets to calls or form submissions, ask why.

A fifteen minute monthly routine

On the first of every month, open your Google Business Profile and write down calls, website clicks, and direction requests for the prior month. Add new reviews received and your current total. Add form submissions from your website. Then open Search Console and note the three searches sending you the most clicks.

Keep it in one simple spreadsheet. Six rows, one per month. After three months you will see a trend instead of a feeling, and you will know whether to keep going, push harder, or change direction.

The bottom line

Local SEO is slow enough that memory is a bad judge of it. Write the numbers down, look at contacts before rankings, and give the work a full quarter before you decide. The businesses that track it are the ones that stick with it long enough to win.
