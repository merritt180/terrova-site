How to Write a Google Business Profile Description That Helps You Rank

Every Google Business Profile has a description field sitting quietly under the photos and reviews, up to seven hundred and fifty characters, and most business owners either leave it blank or fill it with a sentence they wrote in thirty seconds and never looked at again.

That is a missed opportunity. The description will not single handedly put you at the top of the map pack, but it does two things that matter. It gives Google more context about what you do and where you do it, and it gives a customer who is already reading your listing one more reason to call instead of scrolling to the next name. Here is how to actually use it.

What the description field is for

Your Google Business Profile description sits below your reviews and photos on the listing itself. It is not the same as your website copy, and it is not the same as a Google Post, which disappears after a week. The description is a fixed block of text that stays on your profile until you change it.

Google has said plainly that the description does not carry heavy ranking weight on its own. Your category, your reviews, and how close you are to the searcher matter far more. But the description still earns its place for two reasons. It reinforces the services and location signals that live elsewhere on your profile, and it is one of the last things a customer reads before deciding whether to trust you.

The mistake almost every business makes

Open ten Google Business Profiles in your industry and you will see the same pattern. A vague sentence, or a paragraph copied straight from the homepage, that never mentions a city, a service, or a single detail that separates the business from the one next to it.

A dental practice that writes "We provide quality dental care for the whole family" could be describing almost any dentist in the country. That kind of line gives Google nothing new to work with and gives a patient no real reason to choose that office over the three others they are also looking at.

How to structure one that works

A strong description does four things in a short space. It states what the business does, names the city or neighborhood it serves, mentions two or three specific services, and closes with something that builds trust, a credential, a number of years in business, or what makes the experience different.

Here is what that looks like for a few of the industries we work with most.

A dental practice in Studio City: "Studio City Smiles has served families in Studio City and the surrounding San Fernando Valley for over fifteen years. We offer general dentistry, cosmetic dentistry, and same day emergency appointments in a calm, modern office. New patients are always welcome."

A home builder serving Dallas Fort Worth: "We design and build custom homes across the Dallas Fort Worth area, from the first sketch through the final walkthrough. Our team handles architecture, permitting, and construction under one roof, so clients work with a single point of contact from start to finish."

A luxury car rental company in Los Angeles: "We rent exotic and luxury vehicles across Los Angeles and Orange County, from weekend getaways to weddings and photo shoots. Our fleet includes Lamborghini, Ferrari, and Rolls Royce, with white glove delivery available anywhere in the county."

A family law firm: "Our family law attorneys represent clients across the county in divorce, custody, and support matters. We offer a free initial consultation and focus on resolving cases efficiently, without unnecessary court time or expense."

Notice what each one does. City first, specific services next, then one detail that builds confidence. None of them are clever. They are just specific, and specific is what both Google and the customer are looking for.

A few things to avoid

Do not stuff the description with a long list of keywords strung together. Google's own guidelines discourage this, and it reads poorly to an actual customer. Write in full sentences the way you would talk to someone standing in front of you.

Do not include a phone number, website link, or promotional offer. Google's policy does not allow links or calls to action inside the description field, and profiles that include them can be flagged during review.

Do not copy your homepage text word for word. Google can tell when the same paragraph shows up on your website and in dozens of other profiles across the internet. Original text, even if it says something similar, performs better and reads more like a real business.

The bottom line

The description will not make or break your map ranking by itself, but it is one of the easiest fields on your profile to get right, and most competitors have not bothered. Spend fifteen minutes writing one that names your city, your services, and the thing that makes a customer trust you, and retire the vague version for good.
