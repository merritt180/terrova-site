How to Set Up Your Google Business Profile Service Area the Right Way

A home builder in Calabasas does not want calls from Long Beach. A luxury car rental company that delivers to hotels all over Los Angeles does not want to be tied to one street address. Both problems come down to one setting in your Google Business Profile: how you define your service area.

Get it right and Google understands exactly where you work. Get it wrong and you either miss customers you could easily serve, or you attract calls from places you will never go. Here is how it works and how to set it up properly.

Storefront, service area, or both

Google sorts businesses into three types. A storefront business has a location where customers come to you, like a dental practice or a med spa. A service area business travels to the customer, like a pool builder or a mobile detailing company. A hybrid business does both, like a law firm that meets clients at the office and also visits them at home or in the hospital.

Your type decides what shows up on your profile. A pure service area business should hide its street address. A hybrid business shows its address and lists the areas it also serves. Choosing the wrong type is one of the most common reasons a profile gets flagged or suspended.

Where the service area setting lives

Open your Business Profile and choose Edit profile, then Business information, then Service area. You can add areas by city, ZIP code, or neighborhood. Google lets you list up to 20 areas.

One rule matters more than the rest. Google expects your service areas to be within about two hours of driving from your business location. Listing the whole state of California when you work in three cities does not help you. It only makes your profile look careless.

The biggest myth: the service area is not a ranking radius

Many owners believe that adding a city to their service area makes them show up in that city. It does not work that way. The service area tells Google where you are willing to work. Your ranking still depends mostly on how close your actual business location is to the person searching, plus your reviews, your categories, and your website.

A real estate team based in Pasadena can list Glendale and Burbank as service areas, but a searcher standing in Burbank will still tend to see Burbank agents first. The service area is a signal, not a guarantee. To win in the nearby cities, you need real proof on your website that you serve them, which we cover below.

How to choose your areas

Start with where your best customers actually live. Look at your last 20 jobs, closings, or bookings and list the cities. Those are your core areas, and they should come first.

Then add the places you want more work from, as long as you are truly willing to go there. A custom home builder might serve Westlake Village, Agoura Hills, and Calabasas. An exotic car rental company that delivers might list Beverly Hills, West Hollywood, Santa Monica, and Newport Beach, since those are places where customers expect delivery.

Skip anywhere you would turn the job down. A profile that promises more than you deliver leads to wasted calls and unhappy first impressions.

Hiding your address the right way

If customers never visit you, such as a builder working from a home office, you should hide your address. Under Business information, you can choose to hide the address and show only the service area. This protects your privacy and follows Google's rules.

If customers do visit you, do not hide it. A dental practice with a real front door should always show its address, because that is what puts it on the map for people nearby.

Back it up on your website

This is the step most businesses skip, and it is where the real results come from. If you want to be found in a nearby city, your website should have a page that speaks to that city. Not a copy of your home page with the city name swapped in, but a real page with real details. Projects you completed there, questions people in that area ask, and the specific services you offer.

A home builder might have a page about building in Agoura Hills that mentions local permit timelines, hillside lot challenges, and photos of finished homes nearby. That page tells Google and customers the same thing: you work here, and you know the area.

Keep it consistent

The areas on your Google profile should match what your website says, and both should match your directory listings. If your profile lists ten cities but your website mentions only two, Google gets mixed signals. Review all three every few months, especially when you start serving a new area or stop serving an old one.

The bottom line

Your service area setting is small, but it shapes who finds you and who calls. Pick the right business type, list only the places you truly serve, hide your address only when customers never visit, and support every area with real content on your website. Do those four things and Google has a clear picture of your business.
