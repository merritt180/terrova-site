Local SEO for Accounting Firms: How to Be the CPA Clients Find First

Nobody wakes up and browses for an accountant. They get a notice from the IRS, they sell a building, or they finally decide their books are a mess. Then they search "CPA near me" and call one of the first three names they see.

That moment is where accounting firms win or lose new clients. The good news is that most firms in Los Angeles and Orange County are doing almost nothing about it, which leaves a lot of room for a practice that does the basics well. Here is where to start.

Choose your categories like they are your pricing

Your primary category on Google Business Profile tells Google what kind of firm you are. "Accountant" and "Certified Public Accountant" and "Tax Preparation Service" are different categories, and they lead to different searches. A firm that serves business owners should not lead with a category built for individual tax returns.

Pick the one that matches the clients you most want, then add secondary categories for the other work you do. If you handle bookkeeping, payroll, or business consulting, say so there.

Build one page for each service

This is the biggest gap we see. Many firm websites have a single services page with a bulleted list that says tax, audit, bookkeeping, and advisory. Google cannot rank a list. It ranks pages that answer a specific question.

A restaurant group looking for a CPA does not search "accounting services." They search "restaurant accountant in Los Angeles." A dentist buying a second practice searches "CPA for dental practices." A home builder wants someone who understands job costing and work in progress. Each of those deserves its own page, written in plain language, with your city named and a clear next step.

You do not need fifty pages. Start with your three most profitable services and the two or three industries you know best.

Make your niche impossible to miss

The firms that grow fastest usually own a specialty. A practice that says it works with medical offices, real estate investors, or luxury service businesses gives Google and potential clients a reason to choose it over a generalist.

If you already serve a certain type of client, put that on your homepage, in your profile description, and in your page titles. Prospects with bigger budgets are looking for someone who has done this before, and they decide quickly.

Reviews matter more than you think, and they are tricky for CPAs

Accounting is private work, so clients are often uncomfortable posting reviews. That is why many strong firms have only a handful. It is also a real opening. A firm with 40 thoughtful reviews looks like a safe choice next to a competitor with six.

Ask at the right moment. The best time is right after you deliver good news, such as a refund, a clean audit result, or a finished cleanup of messy books. Send a short text or email with a direct link and keep the request simple. Tell clients they do not need to mention numbers or personal details, just what it was like to work with you. And reply to every review, always keeping client information out of your response.

Treat tax season as a local search event

Search traffic for accountants is not flat across the year. It builds in January, peaks in March and April, and spikes again around extension deadlines in the fall. Your profile and website should be ready before those waves hit.

In the weeks before each one, update your hours, add fresh photos of your office and team, post about deadlines and what clients should bring, and check that your booking or contact form works on a phone. A firm that looks active and easy to reach in February gets the call that a quiet profile never sees.

Show the people behind the practice

Clients hand over their finances, so trust decides everything. Your website should have real photos, real names, and short bios that mention credentials, years in practice, and the kinds of clients you enjoy working with. Add your licenses and any memberships. These details help visitors feel comfortable, and they also give Google clearer signals about who you are and where you operate.

Keep your information consistent

Your firm name, address, and phone number should read the same everywhere, from your website to your Google profile to directories like Yelp and your state society listing. Firms that have moved offices or merged names often have old listings quietly confusing Google. A cleanup here is unglamorous, and it pays off.

The bottom line

Accounting clients tend to stay for years, which makes every new one worth a great deal. A strong profile, a page for each service, a clear specialty, and a steady flow of reviews can move a firm into the top three results within a few months. The firms that do this are not louder than their competitors. They are simply easier to find and easier to trust.
