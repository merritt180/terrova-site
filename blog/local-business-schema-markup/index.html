Local Business Schema Markup: What It Is and Whether Your Website Needs It

When you visit a website, you see a logo, a few photos, and some text. Google sees a pile of words and has to guess which ones are your business name, your address, and your phone number. Local business schema markup removes the guessing.

Schema markup is a small block of code on your website that labels your key facts in a format search engines read easily. Visitors never see it. Google does. For an established local business, it is one of the cleaner technical wins available, and it takes less time than most owners expect.

What schema markup actually does

Think of it as a name tag for your website. Without it, Google reads your page and infers that "Suite 210" probably belongs to your address. With it, you state plainly: this is the business name, this is the street address, this is the phone number, these are the hours, and this is the type of business.

That clarity matters most when Google is deciding whether the business on your website is the same business listed in your Google Business Profile. When those two match cleanly, Google trusts the connection. When your website is vague, Google has to work harder to make it, and it does not always get it right.

One honest note. Schema markup is not a magic ranking switch, and Google has said structured data is not a direct ranking factor for local results. What it does is remove confusion and make your pages eligible for richer displays in search. Think of it as good housekeeping that supports everything else you are doing.

What a local business should include

The most useful markup follows a short list of facts. If you keep it focused, you are far less likely to make mistakes.

Your exact business name, address, and phone number. These should match your Google Business Profile word for word, including abbreviations like "Suite" versus "Ste."

Your business type. Schema has specific types, not just a generic "business." A dental practice uses Dentist, a law firm uses LegalService, a luxury rental company uses AutoRental, a med spa can use MedicalBusiness or HealthAndBeautyBusiness, and a real estate team uses RealEstateAgent. A home builder can use GeneralContractor or HomeAndConstructionBusiness. Picking the closest match tells Google exactly what you do.

Opening hours. If you close early on Fridays or keep different hours for consultations, list them accurately.

Your service area and website address. A builder serving the whole San Fernando Valley should say so. An exotic car rental company delivering to hotels across Los Angeles and Orange County should as well.

Links to your other profiles. Your Google Business Profile, Yelp page, and professional associations can be listed as "sameAs" links. This helps Google confirm that all of these pages belong to one real business.

Where it goes on your website

The best place for your main business markup is your home page, and often your contact page too. If you have several locations, each location page should carry its own markup with its own address and phone number. A dental group with offices in Studio City and Encino needs two separate sets of facts, not one blended version.

Beyond that, you can mark up individual pages. A law firm can label its practice area pages. A med spa can label its treatment pages. Start with the main business details first and add more only when the basics are correct.

Common mistakes we see

The biggest problem is markup that does not match reality. If your schema says you are open until 6 and your Google Business Profile says 5, you have created the exact confusion you were trying to remove. Update both whenever hours change, especially around the holidays.

The second problem is copied code. Plenty of businesses install a plugin or paste a template and never change the placeholder details. We have seen markup listing a business in the wrong city because someone forgot to edit it. Always read what the code actually says.

The third problem is marking up things that are not on the page. Google expects your markup to reflect content visitors can really see. If your markup claims reviews or services the page never mentions, you risk a penalty instead of a benefit.

How to check yours in five minutes

Google offers a free tool called the Rich Results Test. Paste in your home page address and it shows you which markup it found and flags any errors. A second free option is the Schema Markup Validator at validator.schema.org, which checks the code against the official standard.

If either tool says it found nothing, your site has no local business markup, and that is a straightforward fix. If it finds something, read the details. Compare the name, address, phone number, and hours to your Google Business Profile. Any difference is worth correcting.

Who should handle it

If your site runs on a common platform, a good SEO plugin can generate the basics, but the settings still need to be filled in correctly and checked. If your site was custom built, a developer or your marketing partner can add it directly in about an hour. Either way, the work is small compared to the clarity you get in return.

The bottom line

Schema markup will not rescue a business with no reviews or an empty Google Business Profile. But once the fundamentals are in place, it gives Google a clear, accurate picture of who you are and where you work. For a practice or company that depends on being found locally, that clarity is worth an hour of setup.
