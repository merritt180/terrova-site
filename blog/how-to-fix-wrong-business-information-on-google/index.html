How to Fix Wrong Business Information on Google

A patient calls the number on your Google listing and reaches a disconnected line. A couple drives to your showroom on a Sunday because Google says you are open. A client searches your firm and finds an address you left four years ago. Wrong information on Google costs you real customers, and most owners have no idea it is happening.

The frustrating part is that you can do everything right and still end up with errors. Google pulls information from many places, and it lets almost anyone suggest changes to your listing. Here is why that happens, how to spot it, and how to fix it so it stays fixed.

Why your Google listing changes without you

Your Google Business Profile is not a page you fully control. Google treats it as a shared record. Customers can click "Suggest an edit" and propose a new phone number, new hours, or even a new category. Google also compares your profile against your website, online directories, and data it collects on its own. When those sources disagree, Google sometimes decides it knows better than you and updates the listing.

Most of the time these changes come from good intentions. A customer thinks your hours are wrong because you closed early one day. An old directory still has your previous suite number. Occasionally, though, a competitor or a spammer makes changes on purpose. Either way, the result is the same: the information people see is not what you entered.

Step 1: Check what Google is actually showing

Do not assume your listing looks the way you set it up. Search your business name on Google and on Google Maps, preferably from a phone that is not logged in to your account. Look carefully at the name, address, phone number, website link, hours, and primary category.

Then log in to your Business Profile. If Google has applied or proposed changes, you will often see a notice about updates from Google or edits waiting for your review.

Step 2: Reject bad edits and correct the details

When you see a change you did not make, review it and reject it if it is wrong. Then edit the field directly so it shows the correct information. Be exact. If your practice is "Suite 210," do not let it read "Ste 2." If your main line is the one patients should call, make sure that is the primary number, not a fax line or a personal cell.

Pay special attention to the business name. Google is strict about names. Adding extra keywords like "Best Med Spa in Beverly Hills" to your name can get your profile suspended, and Google will sometimes strip those words out on its own. Use your real business name exactly as it appears on your signage and paperwork.

Step 3: Fix the sources Google is reading

This is the step most people skip, and it is the reason errors keep coming back. If your old address still lives on Yelp, Apple Maps, Bing, the Better Business Bureau, or an industry directory, Google sees that information and may decide it is correct. You fix the profile, and a few weeks later the old address shows up again.

Make a list of every place your business appears online and update each one to match your Google profile word for word. Start with your own website, especially the footer and contact page. Then work through the major directories and the ones that matter in your industry. A law firm should check Avvo and the state bar listing. A dental practice should look at Healthgrades and insurance provider directories. A home builder should review Houzz and local builder associations.

Step 4: Keep your hours honest, including holidays

Hours are the most common source of customer complaints and the most common reason people suggest edits. If your listing says you are open and someone shows up to a locked door, they are likely to report it, and that report can change your hours for everyone.

Set your regular hours carefully and use the special hours feature for holidays, staff events, and any day you close early. A luxury car rental company that closes for Thanksgiving, or a med spa that shuts down for a training day, should add those dates ahead of time. It takes two minutes and prevents a frustrated customer and a bad edit.

Step 5: Check back every month

Fixing your listing once is not enough. Because anyone can suggest changes, the only way to protect your information is to look at it regularly. Set a reminder to review your profile once a month. Confirm the name, address, phone, website, hours, and category. Read any notices Google has sent. This small habit catches problems before they cost you calls.

What to do when Google will not accept your fix

Sometimes Google keeps reverting your correction. When that happens, it usually means another source is outweighing you. Go back to Step 3 and look harder for the conflicting information. If you recently moved, make sure your new address appears on your website, your signage, and your business documents, since Google may ask you to verify it again.

If you have done all of that and the listing is still wrong, contact Google Business Profile support directly through your dashboard. Be clear and specific, and have proof ready, such as a utility bill or business license showing the correct details.

The bottom line

Your Google listing is often the first thing a new customer sees, and for many it is the only thing they check before they call or drive over. When the information is wrong, you lose those people without ever knowing they tried. Check your listing, correct it, clean up the sources around it, and keep an eye on it every month. It is simple work that protects every other dollar you spend on marketing.
