Google Business Profile Posts: What to Publish and How Often

Most owners set up their Google listing once, fill in the hours, upload a handful of photos, and never open it again. Meanwhile there is a free publishing tool sitting inside that listing that almost nobody uses, and a good post takes about fifteen minutes.

If you have ever wondered why the practice down the street keeps showing up above you on the map, this is often part of the answer. Not the whole answer, but a real part of it.

What a Google post actually is

A post is a short update that appears on your business listing when someone finds you through Google Search or Google Maps. It can include a few lines of text, a photo, and a button such as Book, Call, or Learn More. Think of it as a small announcement pinned to your listing.

Someone searching for a med spa in Encino sees your listing, and right under your hours and reviews is a card that reads "Now booking evening appointments through September." That is a post. It costs nothing, and it is the only piece of your listing you fully control day to day.

Why Google pays attention

Google favors businesses that look alive. A profile that has not been touched in eight months looks like a business that may not still be open, and Google is careful about sending people to businesses that might have closed.

Posts are not a magic ranking lever. They will not rescue a profile with the wrong categories and four reviews. What they do is add fresh, relevant text to a listing Google is already evaluating, and they give a searcher one more reason to pick you instead of the office next door. In a race between two similar businesses, that is often enough.

What to publish

Offers and specials. A med spa announcing a package for the month. A dental practice reminding patients that unused insurance benefits expire at the end of the year. Keep the terms clear and put an end date on it.

News and progress. A home builder posting three photos of a finished custom home and naming the neighborhood. This works quietly well because it puts real project photos and a real location on your listing at the same time.

Specific inventory or services. An exotic car rental company posting the car that just landed on the lot, with the model name written out. People search for the car, not the category. Same idea for a law firm listing a practice area or a real estate team highlighting a new listing.

Events and timely news. An open house, a seminar, a holiday schedule change, a new location opening. Anything with a date attached belongs here.

Write them like a person, not a brochure

Google allows a decent amount of text, but short wins. Forty to eighty words is plenty. Lead with the thing that matters, say where you are, and give one clear next step.

Always attach a photo, and make it a real one. Your space, your team, your finished work. Stock images get scrolled past, and they make an established business look generic. A builder's actual kitchen photo will beat a purchased one every time.

Mention your city naturally in the text. Not "best dentist Studio City Los Angeles Sherman Oaks," which reads like spam and helps nobody. Just write the way you would say it out loud: "Our Studio City office now has Saturday hours twice a month."

How often to post

Once a week is the honest answer. Older posts get pushed down and out of view fairly quickly, so a post from two months ago is doing almost nothing for you today. Weekly keeps something current sitting on your listing at all times.

The practical way to do this is batching. Sit down for an hour at the start of the month, write four posts, and schedule or publish them one at a time. That is far more realistic than trying to think of something clever every Tuesday.

If weekly is not going to happen, twice a month is still meaningfully better than nothing. What does not work is posting five times in one week and then disappearing until spring.

Use the calendar

Seasonal posts are the easiest ones to write because the reason to act is built in. Dental practices have the December insurance deadline. Med spas have the run up to the holidays and wedding season. Home builders have permitting and weather windows. Luxury rental companies have awards season, graduations, and long holiday weekends. Real estate teams have a monthly market update that practically writes itself.

Map these out once, and half your posting year is already planned.

A few things to avoid

Do not stuff keywords, do not post walls of text, and do not recycle the same announcement over and over. Google can reject posts that break its content rules, so keep everything clean, accurate, and about your own business. Skip anything you cannot back up, especially pricing claims and medical or legal promises.

The bottom line

Posting is one of the few local SEO tasks that requires no budget, no developer, and no permission from anyone. It signals to Google that you are open and active, and it gives customers something current to look at in the exact moment they are deciding who to call.

Fifteen minutes a week. Most of your competitors are not doing it.
