Why Your Business Isn't Showing Up on Google Maps (And How to Fix It)

Nearly half of all Google searches have local intent. Someone types "dentist near me" or "exotic car rental Beverly Hills," and Google shows a map with three businesses at the top. If yours is not one of them, those customers are calling someone else.

The good news is that map rankings are not a mystery. Google looks at a short list of signals, and most businesses that stay invisible are failing at the same few things. Here are the five we see most often.

1. Your Google Business Profile is incomplete

Your Google Business Profile is the single biggest factor in whether you appear on the map. Businesses with complete profiles get up to seven times more clicks than those with bare listings. Complete means every field is filled in: hours, categories, services, photos, description, and attributes. Most profiles we audit are less than half done.

The fix: Log in to your profile and fill in every single field. Choose your primary category carefully, because it carries the most weight. A dentist who picks "Dentist" instead of "Cosmetic Dentist" as the primary category will rank differently for different searches.

2. You have too few reviews, or you stopped getting new ones

Reviews are both a ranking signal and a trust signal. 88 percent of consumers trust online reviews as much as personal recommendations. Google also pays attention to how recently reviews come in. A business with 40 reviews from three years ago will often lose to a business with 25 reviews where five arrived this month.

The fix: Build a simple habit of asking every happy customer for a review, ideally the same day. A text with a direct link to your review page works far better than asking people to find you on Google themselves.

3. Your name, address, and phone number do not match across the web

Google cross-checks your business information against directories like Yelp, Yellow Pages, and industry sites. If your address is written three different ways, or an old phone number is still floating around, Google trusts your listing less and your ranking suffers.

The fix: Pick one exact format for your name, address, and phone number, then update every listing to match. It is tedious work, but it is one of the most reliable ways to move up in the map results.

4. Your website does not mention your city or services clearly

Google connects your profile to your website. If your site never says what you do and where you do it in plain text, Google has to guess. A beautiful site that only says "We create smiles" gives Google nothing to work with. A site that says "family dentistry in Studio City" answers the question directly.

The fix: Make sure your city and your core services appear in your page titles, headings, and text. This is often a small edit with an outsized impact.

5. You are ignoring the profile after setup

Google favors active businesses. Profiles that post updates, answer questions, respond to reviews, and add photos regularly send a signal that the business is alive and well. Profiles that sit untouched for months slowly slide down the results.

The fix: Spend 20 minutes a week on your profile. Post a photo, share an update, and reply to every new review, good or bad. Consistency beats intensity here.

The bottom line

Showing up on Google Maps is not about tricks. It is about giving Google complete, consistent, and current information, then staying active. Most businesses can climb noticeably within 60 to 90 days by fixing the five items above.
