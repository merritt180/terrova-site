Local SEO for Dentists: How New Patients Actually Find Your Practice

Almost nobody chooses a dentist the way practices assume they do. There is no careful comparison of credentials. Someone searches "dentist near me," glances at the three offices on the map, reads a few reviews, and calls whichever one feels safest. That decision takes about ninety seconds.

Which means the quality of your dentistry, the equipment you invested in, the years you spent building a reputation, none of it enters the conversation unless you are on that map. Local SEO is simply the work of making sure you are.

The searches that actually matter

Most practices think about one keyword: "dentist" plus their city. That is the biggest search, and it is also the hardest to win, because every office in a five mile radius is fighting for it.

The searches that quietly fill a schedule are more specific. "Emergency dentist open Saturday." "Invisalign near me." "Dental implants Sherman Oaks." "Pediatric dentist that takes Delta Dental." These bring in patients who already know what they want and are ready to book. They also face far less competition, which means a well built profile can win them in weeks rather than years.

Look at the treatments that produce your best revenue, then make sure your online presence actually says you do them. A practice that earns most of its income from implants and cosmetic work but describes itself only as "general dentistry" is invisible to the exact patients it wants.

Your Google Business Profile is your real front door

For a dental practice, the profile does more work than the website. It shows your hours, your photos, your reviews, your phone number, and increasingly your booking link. Many patients never visit your site at all.

Three things move the needle most:

Categories. Your primary category decides which searches you are eligible to appear in. "Dentist" is broad. "Cosmetic Dentist," "Pediatric Dentist," "Dental Implants Periodontist," and "Emergency Dental Service" are all separate categories you can add. Choose the primary one to match your highest value service, then add the rest as secondary.

Services. There is a services section most offices leave empty. Fill it in with plain patient language: teeth cleaning, root canal, veneers, crowns, whitening, Invisalign, emergency care. Each entry is another way Google understands what you offer.

Photos. Patients are nervous about dentistry. Photos of your actual operatories, your waiting area, and your team do more to win a call than any headline. Stock photos of a smiling model do the opposite.

Reviews are the whole ballgame in dental

In most industries reviews matter. In dentistry they decide it. People are choosing who gets to put instruments in their mouth, so they read carefully, and they read the recent ones.

Two practices can be equally good and land in very different places. One has 300 reviews with a steady handful arriving each month. The other has 40 reviews, the newest from two years ago. Google reads that difference as momentum, and so do patients.

The fix is a habit, not a campaign. Every patient who leaves happy after a cleaning or a completed case gets a text before they reach the parking lot, with a direct link to your review page. Same day asks work. A week later, nobody remembers.

Say the neighborhood out loud

Dental websites tend to be beautiful and vague. Soft photography, a line about compassionate care, and no clear statement of what the practice does or where it is. Google cannot rank what it cannot read.

Your homepage title, your headings, and your body text should name the city and the neighborhoods you draw from. A Studio City practice that also serves Sherman Oaks and Toluca Lake should say so in plain words. If you have more than one location, each one needs its own page with its own address, phone number, hours, and map. One combined page for two offices will underperform every time.

Consistency across the web

Dental practices accumulate listings. Health directories, insurance provider pages, Yelp, old marketing vendors, dental association profiles. Over the years, phone numbers change, suites get renumbered, a doctor's name gets added or dropped. Google notices the mismatch and trusts you a little less.

Pick one exact version of your name, address, and phone number, then correct every listing you can find. It is unglamorous work and it reliably moves rankings.

What to expect

A practice that fixes categories and services, cleans up its listings, adds real photos, and starts asking for reviews every day usually sees movement in the map results within sixty to ninety days. The specific searches move first. The broad "dentist near me" ranking takes longer, and it comes as a byproduct of doing everything else well.

None of this is complicated. It is just steady, specific work that most practices never get around to, which is precisely why it still works.
