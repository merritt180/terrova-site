Local SEO for Multiple Locations: How to Rank in Every City You Serve

Opening a second location feels like doubling the business. In Google's eyes, it usually does not work that way. Most owners we talk to have two offices and one of them is invisible, and nobody can explain why.

This happens to dental groups with a Studio City office and an Encino office. It happens to med spas that expanded from West Hollywood into Beverly Hills. It happens to law firms with a downtown suite and a Valley satellite, and to home builders and car rental companies covering half of Southern California from one lot. The pattern is almost always the same, and so is the fix.

Google ranks locations, not companies

The most useful thing to understand is that Google does not rank your business. It ranks each individual location. When someone searches for a service in Encino, Google looks for a business with an address near Encino, a profile that says it serves Encino, and a website page that talks about Encino. Your reputation in Studio City does almost nothing for that search.

So a second location does not inherit anything. It starts near zero and has to earn its own visibility. Owners who expect the new office to ride the coattails of the original one are usually disappointed for about a year.

Every location needs its own Google Business Profile

This sounds obvious and it is still the single most common mistake. One profile with two addresses in the description does not work. Each location needs a separate, verified, fully completed profile with its own address, its own local phone number, and its own hours.

A few details matter more than people expect. Use a distinct phone number for each location if you can, because a shared number across two listings is one of the things that gets profiles suspended. Keep the business name identical across both, with only the city differing if your brand actually uses that format. And make sure each profile has its own photos. Reusing the same twelve interior shots on both listings is a wasted opportunity to show people what they will actually walk into.

Every location needs its own page on your website

If your site has one Contact page listing both addresses, you have given Google one page to rank for two cities. It will pick one and ignore the other.

Build a real page for each location at its own URL. Give it the address, the phone number, the hours, a map, photos of that specific office, the team who works there, and a few paragraphs written about that city in normal language. A med spa page for Beverly Hills should mention Beverly Hills, the streets nearby, the parking situation, and the treatments that location actually offers. Then link each profile to its matching page rather than to your homepage. That one change alone moves the needle for a lot of businesses.

What you should not do is copy the same page twice and swap the city name. Google sees near duplicate pages constantly and gives them very little credit. If you cannot think of 300 honest words about the second location, that is a sign the page is not ready yet.

Reviews do not travel between locations

Reviews attach to a specific profile. Your original office with 180 reviews does not lend a single one to the new location, which is why the new one looks brand new to every customer who finds it. It is brand new.

Treat review generation as a separate campaign at each site, with a separate link and a separate habit. Front desk staff at the new location need their own routine for asking, and the ask has to happen the same day the client is happy. A new location that gathers 20 to 30 real reviews in its first few months looks established. One with four reviews looks like a risk, no matter how strong the parent brand is.

What about a service area with no storefront

Home builders, mobile services, and rental companies that deliver often serve a wide territory from a single address. Google allows a service area business, where you hide your address and list the cities you cover. That is legitimate and it works, but it has limits. You will rank most strongly closest to your actual address, and listing 40 cities does not make you competitive in all 40.

The better move is to pick the three or four cities that actually matter to your revenue and build a genuine page for each one, with projects, photos, and details specific to that area. Depth in four cities beats a thin claim on forty every time.

The bottom line

Multiple locations are not a marketing problem, they are a repetition problem. Each location needs its own profile, its own page, its own reviews, and its own photos. Do that work once per location and each one starts pulling its own weight. Skip it, and you keep paying rent on an office that Google has never really noticed.
